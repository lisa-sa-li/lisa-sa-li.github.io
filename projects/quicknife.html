Timing the Throw in a Quick Knife Draw Competition

February - April 2018

A request for proposal (RFP) is a document outlining an opportunity, and the framing, objectives, criteria, and constraints needed in a solution. For one of my first year engineering design courses, I worked on a 3 month project in which my team and I wrote an entire proposal, designed a solution, and built a functioning prototype.


Our RFP was regarding the knife throwing community, a group of individuals who throw knives for recreation and sport. While interviewing members of the community, they addressed the need of a accurate timing mechanism to measure the time of their throws.


One set of knife throwing competitions is the quick draw, where two throwers must draw their knives and hit the target faster than the other. The concern is that in competitions with high skilled throwers, the knives hit the target within a fraction of a second from each other, making it impossible to tell by sound or sight who the winner is. Through several interviews, in-depth field tests, and research of the sport and similar products, we were able to compose an RFP that was one of the few selected to continue on to the prototype phase to be displayed and demonstrated at an end-of-year showcase.


The objectives of the RFP were to:


  1. Accurately record the duration of a knife throw, from when timer begins to when the knife hits the board.
  2. Indicate the start of each round without causing disadvantages for players with various throwing preferences.
  3. Build a strong and durable device that can handle knife hits as well as damage due to outdoor exposure.
  4. Create a portable product that can easily be transported for competitions

Our proposed solution, the QuicKnife, addresses the needs of a quick draw competition. Our device featured:


  • A piezoelectric sensor connected to an LED screen to display the time and 3 colored lights to indicate the beginning and end of each round.
  • A pair of connected devices to compare speeds and indicate the faster time between the two
  • A remote control for the referee to turn on the device or reset the time for each round.
  • A stainless steel body and polycarbonate frontal cover, which were tested to be durable enough to withstand the blow of a knife without shattering or breaking.
  • A light-weight and small device totalling at 1 kg each and is approximately 16x10x10 cm.

Programming and circuitry, material selection and testing was the largest part of our project. Building our prototypes taught me many hands on skills. For the opportunity, we learned how to throw knives, which came in handy as for one of our tests, we threw knives against various kinds of materials to test their strength. We also spent hours in workshops bending, drilling, and cutting materials. I also learned how to program an Arduino, as that was the microcontroller that powered our device, and creating the circuits applied the knowledge I learned in my ECE course. My team and I spent several hours solely on building the device, and it paid off as it worked! Check out the video below.
