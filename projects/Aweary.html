Aweary

September 2019

While participating at RBC’s hackathon AmpHacks 2019, they challenged us with the prompt “How can you improve Canadian lives?”. Our app placed 3rd overall in the competition with over 200 participants!


In the summer of 2019, after waking up to an empty GO train and getting off seconds before the doors shut, I had trouble falling asleep during my commute for the rest of summer for a fear of that happening again. That sucked because that 45 minute commute would have been a great way to catch up on sleep.


This experience came to me when I heard this prompt, leading my team to deliver Aweary, a proof-of-concept mobile app to wake users up, and not those sitting next to them, on their commute.


Our application used a combination of GPS location tracking and accelerometer to track your vehicle’s position relative to your destination. Why an accelerometer? By tracking forward acceleration in a moving vehicle, the algorithm will determine whether the vehicle has stopped moving as well as the total distance travelled. The application will use this information to notify you once you’ve reached your destination. Uber and Google’s Alarm app were inspirations for the design.


The business implications of our app was thought out and presented. It is a huge benefit for RBC to know the travelling patterns of their customers. By tracking users’ travelling routes, we are able to create user behavioral patterns to help design/ innovation banking products.


The app was designed for 3 groups of people

  1. Impulsive: e.g. tourists, they’re unfamiliar to the area and must be cautious the entire ride for their stop
  2. Habitual: provide them a relaxing ride so they can get distracted in reading a book or take a nap
  3. People with disabilities such as vision or hearing impairment

Torontonians on average spend 96 minutes commuting everyday and people want to be productive but in doing so, in our subconscious we risk missing our stop and wasting more time. Our app aimed to improve Canadian lives by giving this time back to commuters for them to do work, catch up on sleep, and spend time with loved ones.


UI Design Decisions

  • For our branding, I decided on a dark shade of blue-grey, achieving a dark mode look for our sleep app. A magenta shade was selected to offset the darkness, but is used minimally as to not overwhelm the user with pink.
  • The dashboard highlights the upcoming alarm, offering a reminder to users
  • Labels for alarms to easily to distinguish them
  • The ability to favorite alarms, meant for common commutes such as work
  • Having the app play an alarm only if the user has headphones in, in order to not disturb the rest of the riders. The app will vibrate otherwise.
  • Allowing users to schedule when to notify them before they reach their destination, and the option to cancel the alarm while on their commute
