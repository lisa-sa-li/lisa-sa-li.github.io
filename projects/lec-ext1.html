Lecture Extension Report: The Dependence of Presentation Slides and Its Affect in Praxis Lectures

January 2018

My lecture extension was on the over-dependence of PowerPoint presentations. It was inspired by my professor who analyzed a poorly designed report by Boeing engineers in the form of 3 PowerPoint presentations that led to the explosion of the space shuttle Columbia in 2003. I was strongly interested in this topic as something as simple as a PowerPoint presentation is another design project in my eyes.


I read a book authored by a professor at Yale, Edward Tufte, who criticized the use of PowerPoint slides due to issues such as text size, misuse of bullet points, inconsistent notation, and misleading vocabulary, all leading NASA to believe that there were no major concerns.


I then analyzed the use of PowerPoints in one of my design courses, Praxis, which I found to be destructive to my learning as "due to the non - linear appearance of text bubbles, as well as the use of overlapping bubbles on top of text , uploaded slides are often over 40 pages long . The issue, as explained by Tutfe, is that audiences 'endure a relentless sequentially.' " (from my extension). I then compared it to slides from my physics lectures which used slides to enhanced the knowledge of the courses by having slides with "visual reasoning".


This is an example as to why I design for aesthetics. I consider color contrast and font size as beautiful slides encourage me to read them. This was an issue that I, as well as my peers, have with Praxis lectures, and I believe addressing this will benefit future Engineering Science students in their learning of Praxis and engineering communication.
